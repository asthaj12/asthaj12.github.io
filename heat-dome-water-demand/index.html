Heat-Dome
Demand Forecast

Where, when and how much water demand moves in a heat wave

Astha built a 7-day forecast for a heat dome over Texas and the Southwest, where demand followed how far heat ran above each region's normal and how many days in a row it held, not the absolute temperature. She pre-built 136,000 cases inside the bottler's lead-time gap, allocated them by cluster, and lifted peak in-stock from 84% to 96%.

01 · Problem

Heat looked like one national number. Demand did not.

Demand depended on how far heat ran above each region's normal and on how many days in a row it held. The bottler's surge also arrived after the warning.

Problem A · Where and when

The same 106°F high meant different things in two places

Dallas-Fort Worth had a normal late-July high of 96 and a dome high of 106, an anomaly of +10. Phoenix had a normal high of 106 and the same dome high, an anomaly of 0. A national temperature forecast reads both days the same, so it sizes Phoenix for a heat event that did not happen there.

Demand also did not respond to the first hot day. It moved on the third day in a row.

Dallas anomaly +10 Phoenix anomaly 0 Responds from day 3
Problem B · Lead time

The warning came 7 days out. The bottler needed 10.

A surge ordered on the warning would land 10 days later, 3 days after the heat arrived. Normal bottler supply was 400,000 cases a week, and the peak week needed about 607,000.

Waiting for the surge would leave the stores short during those 3 days, and short again for the rest of the peak week after it landed.

7-day warning 10-day lead time 3 days uncovered
02 · Decision

What to forecast on, and how much to pre-build

Each decision changed the question before any stock was ordered.

Decision A · Forecast driver

Forecast on anomaly × duration, by cluster

Diagnose

Astha diagnosed the first model's error. It used absolute temperature and over-forecast Phoenix by 67%. Phoenix's 106°F dome high was its normal, so the model treated a normal day as a heat event. Switching to anomaly by cluster fixed it.

Decide
✕ Rejected: absolute temperature with one national uplift, the same % for every store. That sends Phoenix stock it does not need and starves Dallas.
✓ Picked: anomaly against each region's own normal, times a duration factor, with a response per degree set for each cluster.
Astha owned the water plan Peak-week error +18% → ~4%
Decision B · Pre-build and allocation

Pre-build 136K cases to the 80th percentile and allocate by cluster share

Diagnose

Astha diagnosed the lead-time gap. The surge would land 3 days after the heat, leaving about 88,850 cases uncovered over those days. Even after it landed, supply stayed 47,320 cases a week short of the peak-week need.

Decide
✕ Rejected: wait for the bottler surge. It lands 3 days after the heat.
✕ Rejected: pre-build to the average case or the heavy case. The average leaves stores short in every outcome above it, and the heavy case carries more stock than the 80th percentile needs.
✓ Picked: pre-build to the 80th percentile and split it by each cluster's share of peak-week demand. Water keeps and sells in the recovery week, so overstock is cheap and a stockout loses sales.
Astha owned the water plan 136K cases pre-built
03 · Method

Where, when and how much demand moves

Three effects set each cluster's response. Name each one before setting a stock number.

Plain-words version

The forecast reads heat as a departure from normal, not as a temperature. It asks how far above the local normal the high is, whether the heat has lasted long enough to move buying, and how much each cluster buys per degree of heat. A 106°F day is a major event in Dallas and a normal day in Phoenix.

Where
Anomaly vs local normal
The dome high minus the 30-year normal high for that date and place. Dallas: 106 − 96 = +10. Phoenix: 106 − 106 = 0, until the dome reached 110 (+4) late in the week.
When
Duration factor
Day 1 of a run of hot days counts 0, day 2 counts 0.5, and day 3 onward counts 1.0. Demand moved on the third day in a row, not the first.
How much
Cluster response per degree
Dallas responds at 0.15 per degree of anomaly, so +10 gives 1 + 0.15 × 10 = 2.5x on full-effect days.
Multiplier1 + response × anomaly × duration
Store, normal week40 cases a day × 7 days = 280 cases
Dallas-Fort Worth240 × 280 × 2.5 = 168,000 cases
Houston210 × 280 × 2.1 = 123,480 cases
Phoenix180 × 280 × 1.2 = 60,480 cases
Rest of Texas570 × 280 × 1.6 = 255,360 cases

Rule from the check

Plan the next dome's lift as mostly incremental, about 90%. Trim the recovery week by about 8% instead of assuming it returns to normal right away. The dip is the pulled-forward stock: households were using pantry stock bought during the dome.

05 · Transfer

Why it matters for capacity planning

These principles come from the heat dome. They apply to any capacity commitment made before the demand is known.

The principle

Capacity and long-lead supply are committed before the real shape of demand is visible. A signal that mixes local conditions, early buying and a slow supply response sizes the commitment for demand that will not repeat. Measure each signal against its own baseline, wait for it to persist, and split it before sizing.

Principle 01
Measure against the local normal
A region's demand signal means something only against its own baseline. The same temperature can be an event in one place and a normal day in another.
Principle 02
Wait for persistence before committing
Demand responded on the third day in a row, not the first. Commit once the signal has held, not on the first forecast of a hot day.
Principle 03
Split incremental from pulled forward
Before sizing the next commitment, separate new consumption from demand bought early. Here about 90% of the lift was new and about 10% was pulled forward.
Principle 04
Close the lead-time gap
When the lead time is longer than the warning, pre-build the gap or reserve an option in advance. Here the gap was 3 days.
Principle 05
Allocate by share of the peak
Give each region its share of the peak-week need, not one national uplift that sends stock to places that do not need it.
