01 · Problem

Where Excess Enters

Each entry point looks reasonable on its own signal. Each fails when that signal is not checked before commitment.

Long-lead hardware and retail buys are committed well before the product sells through. At the moment of commitment, the signal in hand is usually sell-in orders, a prior-year peak, a last-time-buy assumption or a blanket uplift. Each of these can be a reasonable input and still produce excess when nobody tests it against what customers are actually doing.

Entry 01
Demand signal
Sell-in orders can look like demand while consumer sell-through has slowed. A build sized on a prior-year surge inherits that surge.
Failure: build sized to sell-in, not sell-through
Entry 02
Product transition
A last-time buy that assumes customers stay on the old model overstates the old model's demand. The successor absorbs part of it.
Failure: one write-off number per transition
Entry 03
Design change
A revision that lands close to pilot build can leave pilot parts on the old revision in the plan.
Failure: pilot tested on parts that will not ship
Entry 04
Event
A blanket uplift credited to trend turns one event's lift into a baseline that the next purchase order inherits.
Failure: purchase orders sized to uplift the data does not support

03 · Method

The Excess Prevention Checklist

One check per entry point, run before inventory is bought, built, or credited to trend.

Read left to right. A check that does not pass holds the commitment.

05 · Transfer

Why It Matters for Long-Lead Planning

The same discipline applies wherever lead times are long and commitments come early.

Stranded inventory is the long-lead equivalent of stranded equipment or capex. Once a long-lead part is bought against a signal that does not hold, the cost is committed and the part has no buyer. The checks in these cases carry over to any long-lead plan.

Transfer 01
Validate demand before supply
Test the signal against what sells through, what sits in channel and what is actually used before a long-lead commitment is cut, not after the order is confirmed.
Transfer 02
Sequence design changes
Run a where-used check on every affected part, and agree with Engineering and Operations which build proceeds on which revision before pilot or production commitments.
Transfer 03
Time transitions to end of life
Net last buys for expected substitution and set end-of-life dates by channel, so supply stops where demand stops.
What transfers is the check, not the numbers. Each result depends on its own lead time, channel mix and data. The figures on this page are rounded and illustrative.
