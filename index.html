Running Consumer Hardware Launches From NPI Through End-of-Life

Logitech · Consumer hardware · 2020–2022

  • NPI → EOL lifecycle
  • Kinaxis MRP/MPS
  • ODM & contract manufacturing
  • Safety stock & allocation
  • Cross-functional alignment
Problem
A ~$400M global portfolio of 20+ product lines and 400+ SKUs, built by Asia-based ODM partners on long lead times. Factories, ODMs, sourcing and regional demand teams each owned a piece of the launch date; no one owned all of it.
My Ownership
Global supply planning and launch execution from new product introduction to end-of-life, on a weekly MRP/MPS cadence, aligning factories, ODMs, sourcing and regional demand teams on one plan.
Key Decisions
Treated launch readiness as a supply gate (material secured, capacity committed, regional demand fillable) rather than a calendar date; ran a weekly constraint list to closure with factories, ODMs and sourcing; sized safety stock with stochastic models and stated allocation rules.
Result
Apple Education Program launch delivered ~3 weeks ahead of schedule (~15% acceleration); +40% finished-goods availability; ~$200K premium air freight and ~$1M/yr excess & obsolete inventory cost removed.

Planning Decision & Forecast Governance System

Walmart US · Retail grocery · 2023–present

  • Exception triage
  • Forecast governance
  • Planning reviews
  • Program management
  • Cross-functional adoption
Problem
More than 50,000 forecast and replenishment exceptions arrived each week as an unranked list; diagnosing one item took 20+ minutes; and nothing measured whether planners' manual overrides helped or hurt.
My Ownership
The governance rules and audit trail, the exception-triage process, the weekly exception review, and forecast diagnostics across ~130,000 products. Data Science owns the forecast engine; Replenishment owns the buy.
Key Decisions
Reframed "make the forecast better" into "make the decisions on top of the forecast visible, ranked and accountable"; joined machine forecast, planner adjustments, replenishment output and actual demand into one record; led with diagnostic value so accountability followed.
Result
Per-item diagnosis cut from 20+ minutes to under 3; $7.6M in anomalous replenishment adjustments flagged across 34,952 products; previously unmeasured overrides brought under governance for 4,593 stores. Recognized with a Making a Difference Award.

Network & Capacity Optimizer

Independent project · Python (PuLP) · Excel Solver · Synthetic data

  • Linear & integer programming
  • Stochastic capacity planning
  • Shadow prices
  • Excel Solver
Problem
Three planning decisions made under constraint: where to add peak network capacity, how much long-lead capacity to commit before demand is known, and who gets supply when it is 30% short.
My Ownership
Built end to end: three optimization models in Python (PuLP), the allocation model in Excel Solver, and the charts.
Key Decisions
Scored every plan on cost and risk, and priced each scarce resource with shadow prices so every trade-off has a dollar value.
Result
Showed carrier capacity, not building space, limits peak fill; 25% capacity headroom adds $2.2M of stranded capacity to avoid 3 short months; every fill commitment priced per unit.

Demand Signal Backtesting & Targeting

Walmart US · Retail · $3.1B pilot portfolio

  • Holdout backtesting
  • Time series
  • Intervention targeting
  • Business case
Problem
The weekly-grain ML forecast smoothed over predictable intraweek demand spikes tied to benefit-issuance cycles; residual autocorrelation showed the bias was persistent, not random.
My Ownership
The business logic, temporal targeting criteria and backtest design. Data Science and Engineering owned the technical build.
Key Decisions
Backtested at store × SKU × day grain; stopped the 42% of legacy corrections aimed where on-hand inventory was already sufficient; kept Day-0 interventions and dropped Day-3+ ones that were doing harm.
Result
309 bps wMAPE improvement on the pilot; ~$95M of demand repositioned; became the business case for a complete system redesign.
