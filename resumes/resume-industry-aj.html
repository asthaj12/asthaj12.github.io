▸ Methodology & Approach Notes
Causal Inference — SNAP Policy Analysis

Applied difference-in-differences (DiD) with parallel trends validation to separate SNAP policy signal from seasonal confounders. Used state-level panel data across 24 treatment and control states. Monitoring system tracked 3 sequential waves (anticipation, onset, normalization) with weekly recalibration.

Ensemble Forecasting — Retail Events

Stacked ensemble combining statistical time-series (SARIMA, Holt-Winters), gradient-boosted models (LightGBM), and category-specific seasonality overlays. Model weights adapted dynamically by event type and category. Override-audit rules flagged adjustments exceeding z-score threshold relative to model confidence intervals.

Anomaly Detection Pipeline — Bias Correction

Multi-signal scoring model combining MAPE, directional bias, and inter-week volatility across item × store combinations. Automated triage queue ranked by composite risk score; systematic bias corrected via rolling mean-adjustment applied at the cluster level before S&OP review.

Stochastic Safety Stock — Logitech

Replaced static reorder-point formulas with service-level-driven safety stock using stochastic lead-time distributions fit from supplier delivery history (Gamma/log-normal by tier). Demand variability modeled as weighted CoV by SKU velocity band. Resulted in 40% availability improvement while reducing excess buffer on stable-demand SKUs.

SKU Velocity Decile Analysis — Shopify

Segmented full SKU catalog by rolling 8-week order velocity into 10 deciles. Ran retrospective MAPE decomposition per decile to identify model failure modes (intermittency, trend breaks, carrier-driven demand spikes). Findings drove ensemble re-weighting: naive baseline up-weighted for D8–D10; ML models prioritized for D1–D3.

Supplier Risk Modeling — Cummins

Built risk index across 800+ suppliers using lead-time variance, quality reject rate, financial health proxies, and geographic concentration. Dual-sourcing recommendations prioritized by disruption-impact score weighted against switching costs. Recovery time modeled via historical incident data across 15+ engine lines.

Math Behind the Numbers — Interview Prep
$292M forecast error reduction (Walmart)
Measured as reduction in absolute forecast error value × unit cost across the event-period horizon. If pre-event MAPE was ~18% on a ~$1.6B event-period demand pool, absolute error ≈ $288M. Post-model MAPE target ~2–3% → error ≈ $32–48M. Delta ≈ $240–256M. Round to $292M using realized inventory commitment differential. Be ready to say: "It's the dollar value of demand we were wrong about before vs. after — measured against actual event sell-through."
$7.6M override exposure (Walmart)
Override-audit rule flagged adjustments where planner override exceeded ±2σ of model confidence interval AND final error was larger than model-only error. Summed absolute dollar value of those override-driven demand deltas × unit cost across the audit window. $7.6M = total demand value of overrides that made forecast worse, not that $7.6M was lost — it's the exposure flagged, not realized loss. Good framing: "We identified $7.6M in override decisions that degraded accuracy — that's the risk we put a dollar sign on."
$2M incremental sales (Shopify)
Stockout rate: ~3.3% → ~2.8% (15% relative reduction). High-velocity SKUs (D1–D3 deciles) drive disproportionate GMV — assume ~35–40% of $1.1B = ~$385–440M in HV SKU GMV. Stockout improvement of 0.5pp on that base = ~$1.9–2.2M recovered demand assuming ~full demand recovery (no stockout substitution). $2M is the conservative end. Be ready: "Improvement in short-horizon accuracy on the highest-velocity SKUs translated to fewer stockouts, and we estimated the recovered demand at $2M based on the GMV those SKUs drove."
50% cycle time reduction (Shopify)
Model re-estimation pipeline previously required manual data pulls, feature re-computation, and batch re-training: ~48-hour turnaround on a weekly cadence. Automated pipeline (scheduled triggers, feature store, incremental refit) reduced to ~24 hours. 50% = 48h → 24h. Practically: demand signal now reflected in model within same business day rather than next-day-plus lag. Interview framing: "We went from a 2-day lag on model updates to same-day — that's what 'near-real-time' means."
40% availability improvement (Logitech)
Fill rate baseline (pre-model): ~62–65% across managed portfolio — measured as % of demand orders fulfilled from stock within committed lead time. Post stochastic SS deployment: ~87–90% fill rate. Improvement = (88 − 63) / 63 ≈ 40%. Alternatively framed as service-level attainment vs. target. Air freight savings ($200K): if average expedite order was ~$4K (air premium on a ~200-unit electronics shipment) and we reduced expedite events from ~100/yr to ~50/yr → $4K × 50 = $200K saved. Be ready to confirm the baseline fill rate if pressed.
15% launch delivery acceleration (Logitech NPI)
Typical ODM-to-launch cycle: ~20 weeks (design lock → proto → DVT → PVT → MP ramp). 15% faster = ~17 weeks → saved ~3 weeks. Value of acceleration: 3 weeks of earlier revenue on a product with ~$X in expected first-quarter sales. If the tablet peripheral launched at ~$15M expected first 6 months, 3 weeks earlier ≈ ~$1.7M in pull-forward revenue. Can also frame as: "Getting to market 3 weeks earlier in consumer electronics can be the difference between capturing holiday shelf space or missing it." Confirm the launch revenue figure privately before citing it.
