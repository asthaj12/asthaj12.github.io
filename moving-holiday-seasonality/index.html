Case Study · Seasonal & Holiday Demand Planning

Moving-Holiday
Seasonality Model

Holiday timing was being read as growth. Re-indexing history to the holiday, and separating timing from real growth before committing long-lead buys, cut seasonal plan error from 6 points to 0.4 points at Walmart US Food & Grocery.

01 · Problem

Two holiday-timing effects that looked like growth

Both moved with the holiday, not with the season. Calendar-week planning read the move as demand.

Problem A · Lent & Easter

Easter moves up to five weeks from year to year

Indexed on calendar weeks, spring demand looked 9% higher. Six of those nine points were timing, not growth: Easter landing earlier pulled demand into the window. Only the rest was real growth.

Planning on calendar weeks would have built phantom post-Easter stock, inventory sized for a lift that was mostly the holiday moving.

+9% apparent spring lift 6 of 9 pts timing Easter shifts up to 5 wks
Problem B · Thanksgiving to Christmas

Half of a 6% Q4 beat was pull-forward

Q4 holiday demand beat forecast by 6%. Half of that beat was pull-forward: demand that arrived earlier than the forecast expected. Only the other half was real growth.

The reflex, raising every under-forecast item, would treat that timing as growth and overbuy wherever the beat was only early.

6% beat vs forecast 3 pts pull-forward 3 pts real growth
02 · Decision

What changed in the plan, and who approved it

Each decision changed the question before any buy was placed.

Decision A · Lent & Easter

Re-index history to weeks before Easter

Diagnose

Astha diagnosed the calendar-timing effect. The spring lift followed Easter's date, not the season: six of the nine points were timing, so calendar-week history was measuring where the holiday fell.

Decide
✕ Rejected: plan on calendar weeks. Keeps the +9% signal, including six points of timing.
✓ Picked: re-index to weeks before Easter. Years line up on the holiday, so only real growth carries into the plan.
Senior leadership signed off Plan error 6 → 0.4 pts
Decision B · Q4 holiday peak

Raise only items with a real days-of-supply gap

Diagnose

Half of the 6% beat was pull-forward. Raising the forecast by the full beat would add timing and call it growth.

Decide
✕ Rejected: raise every under-forecast item. Overbuys every item whose beat was only early.
✓ Picked: raise only items with a real days-of-supply gap. The extra buy goes only where stock is actually short.
Astha recommended; senior leadership approved ~$339M unneeded buys avoided
03 · Method

How the model separates timing from growth

Three effects sit inside every holiday week. Name each one before setting a buy.

Plain-words version

Event-relative indexing lines each year up on the holiday instead of the calendar. Ask what happened in the weeks before Easter, not what happened in calendar week 14. A moved holiday then lands in the same place every year, so the gap that remains between years is real growth.

Effect 01
Timing shift
The holiday moved, so its demand moved with it. It looks like growth on calendar weeks and disappears once the years are aligned on the holiday.
Effect 02
Pull-forward
Demand that arrived earlier than the forecast expected. It is real, but it was going to happen anyway, so it does not raise the run-rate.
Effect 03
Structural run-rate growth
The underlying trend that should carry into next year's plan. Only this part should size a long-lead commitment.
Lent & Easter+9 pts observed = 6 pts timing + 3 pts structural growth
Q4 holiday peak+6% beat = 3 pts pull-forward + 3 pts structural growth

05 · Transfer

Why it matters for long-lead planning

Seasonal and timing effects distort the demand signal. The same separation applies to any commitment made before demand is known.

The principle

Long-lead supply, whether inventory, production capacity, or a supplier commitment, is locked in before the season's real shape is visible. If the signal mixes timing and pull-forward with real growth, the commitment is sized to demand that will not repeat, and the result is stranded inventory or idle capacity. Separating timing from real growth first keeps the commitment on the part of the signal that repeats.

Practice 01
Index on the driver
Any demand that moves with a date, such as holidays, school calendars, or weather windows, is aligned on that driver before years are compared.
Practice 02
Split before you size
Timing shift, pull-forward, and structural growth are separate numbers. Only the structural part should size a long-lead buy or capacity reservation.
Practice 03
Act on gaps, not beats
A beat against forecast is a question to answer. Days of supply shows which items are actually short and which were only early.
