Shortage Waterfall — What Limits Completed Systems?

Each component caps buildable servers at ⌊committed ÷ per-server quantity⌋. Buildable is the minimum across parts, capped at demand. The part with the lowest cap is the binding constraint.

Newsvendor Commit Sizing by Component

Underage cost Cu is margin lost per unit of component short. Overage cost Co is carrying cost per unit left over. The critical ratio sets the quantile of each component's demand distribution, and that quantile is the commit to place before server demand is known.

Methodology: Formulas and Limits

Requirement R_i = q_i × D, D = 5,000 servers (mean demand) Attainment A_i = C_i ÷ R_i Per-part buildable B_i = ⌊C_i ÷ q_i⌋ Supply limit S = min_i B_i, binding part = argmin_i B_i Buildable Buildable = min(D, S), commit attainment = Buildable ÷ D Alternate part change one C_k, recompute S with the same min rule non-binding part k: S and Buildable do not move (+0) Next constraint min over the other parts once the binding part is relieved
Demand for parts μ_i = q_i × 5,000, σ_i = q_i × 750 (server demand σ = 750) Critical ratio CR_i = Cu_i ÷ (Cu_i + Co_i) Commit quantile Q_i* = μ_i + σ_i · Φ⁻¹(CR_i) Linked scenario committing the GPU to Q* feeds back into the supply limit above

  • Single-level BOM with no yield, lead-time offsets or multi-level assemblies. Timing is shown as a schedule, not solved.
  • All parts scale with one server-demand driver. Their shortfalls are perfectly correlated, so each Q* is sized on its own marginal and not jointly.
  • Cu and Co are synthetic per-unit penalties. They are not quoted costs.
  • Attainment uses mean demand. The newsvendor uses the demand distribution.
